INTERNAL REVIEW NOTES

Design and content notes for this local build.

This page is included for local review only and is not intended to be part of the public portfolio. The public-facing Home and About pages now use finished portfolio copy rather than recommendation language.

01 · POSITIONING

Lead with engineering scope

The original homepage calls you a “Full-stack Web Developer,” while the deeper site shows systems integration, security, identity, enterprise applications, UX, and AI. The revised hero positions you as a software engineer with systems-integration depth.

02 · INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Selected work first

The original home experience asks visitors to navigate a large carousel. The revised home selects six stronger projects, while the full 18-project archive remains one click away with filters.

03 · PERFORMANCE

Remove the 21 MB hero video

The original hero background video is one of the largest public-facing assets. The review build replaces it with lightweight CSS and markup so the first screen is faster and calmer.

04 · COPY

Make impact easier to scan

Several original descriptions are conversational or contain typos. The revised cards use shorter, outcome-oriented copy. Case studies should eventually add concrete results: time saved, adoption, user count, performance gains, support reduction, or risk reduced.

05 · ACCESSIBILITY

Improve structure and interaction

The redesign adds a skip link, consistent heading hierarchy, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, semantic links instead of buttons nested inside links, and clearer navigation states.

06 · SEO

Use descriptive titles and metadata

The original top-level pages reuse the generic title “Ramiro Puentes.” Revised pages have role/project-specific titles and descriptions. Before deployment, add canonical URLs and Open Graph images tied to the final domain.

HIGH PRIORITY NEXT

Turn case studies into proof of engineering impact.

Your project depth is the strongest part of the site. The next improvement is not more visual polish—it is evidence. For the top 4–6 case studies, add architecture, constraints, your exact responsibilities, security/integration decisions, and measurable outcomes.
